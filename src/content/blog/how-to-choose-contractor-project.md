Selecting the right contractor is arguably the single most consequential decision a property owner makes during an interior design, renovation, or architectural project. While conceptual 3D renders can make any project look enticing, the translation of those drawings into tangible reality depends entirely on the technical skill, trade coordination, and commercial honesty of the contracting team.

Unfortunately, many property owners choose contractors solely based on the lowest initial quote—only to face cost overruns, substandard core materials, and prolonged project delays down the road.

This practical checklist outlines the five critical criteria every property owner should evaluate before engaging an interior or turnkey contractor.

---

## 1. Transparency in the Bill of Quantities (BOQ)

A professional contractor provides a line-item, comprehensive Bill of Quantities (BOQ) rather than a vague lump-sum figure.

### Red Flags to Avoid

- **Lump-Sum Per-Square-Foot Rates**: Quotes that simply state *"₹1,400 per sq. ft. all-inclusive"* without itemizing material specifications or scope boundaries hide massive variation. Crucial items like surface preparation, electrical point re-routing, hardware grades, and debris removal are frequently excluded and billed later as "extra work."
- **Missing Brand Specifications**: Watch out for vague descriptions like *"commercial plywood"* or *"good quality hardware."* 

### What You Should Demand

Insist on explicit material grades and manufacturer names in your contract:
- Plywood core: Calibrated BWR / BWP Marine Grade (e.g., CenturyPly, Greenply, or equivalent IS 710/303 certified).
- Hardware: Specified brands and models (e.g., Hafele, Hettich, or Ebco with full-extension soft-close mechanisms).
- Laminates / Veneers: Minimum 1.0 mm thickness from reputed manufacturers (e.g., Merino, Greenlam).
- Paint: Specific premium emulsion systems with multi-coat putty and primer undercoats.

---

## 2. On-Site Supervision & Trade Management

A contractor is only as dependable as their site management infrastructure. Many small-scale contractors take on too many projects simultaneously, leaving daily work to unsupervised subcontractors.

Ask the following questions during your interview:
1. **Who will be on site daily?** Is there a dedicated site supervisor or project engineer overseeing quality checks?
2. **How are overlapping trades scheduled?** Do civil, plumbing, electrical, and carpentry trades work according to a written Gantt chart, or do they wait for one another?
3. **What is the protocol for drawing clarifications?** If a site dimension differs from the architectural drawing, who makes the decision to adjust?

---

## 3. Financial Terms & Milestone-Linked Payments

A contractor's payment schedule is a direct reflection of their financial health and operational integrity.

- **Reasonable Mobilization Advance**: A standard mobilization advance should typically range between **10% and 20%** of the contract value—enough to procure raw materials and mobilize on-site labor.
- **Stage-Linked Milestones**: Subsequent disbursements should strictly tie to physical, verifiable milestones (e.g., 20% upon completion of civil and electrical rough-ins, 25% upon completion of woodwork carcase assembly, 25% upon surface finishes and stone installation, and 10% on final handover).
- **Retention Money**: Reserve a final **5% to 10%** retention amount payable 30 to 60 days after handover, ensuring minor snagging items are promptly resolved.

---

## 4. Past Client References & Physical Site Inspections

Never rely exclusively on digital photo albums or social media imagery. High-quality 3D renders can easily be passed off as completed sites.

- **Request Access to an Ongoing Site**: Visiting an active project site tells you more about a contractor than any portfolio. Look for clean debris management, organized material stacking, worker safety gear, and edge-banding precision.
- **Speak Directly With Previous Clients**: Ask former clients about schedule adherence, billing transparency, and how the contractor responded when unexpected issues arose during construction.

---

## 5. Post-Handover Warranty & Defect Liability Period

High-quality contracting work is backed by a written commitment. Reputable contractors provide a formal **Defect Liability Period (DLP)**—typically 6 to 12 months post-handover.

This covers:
- Alignment adjustments on cabinet doors and drawer slides as wood settles.
- Grout touch-ups and silicone seal checks around sinks and wet areas.
- Minor settlement hairline crack touch-ups in drywall or plaster joints.
